HOW WE PLAN THE ACTUAL STORY
STAGE 1 — Anchor: current FWFS weekday/date (actual calendar date only if source-verified), location, who is home/away, latest character knowledge, earlier unfinished promises, and one clear ordinary reason for encounter.
STAGE 2 — Event map: for every selected source beat, record source ID + exact event name + owner POV + sequence + setting + doer + affected character + witness + consequence + conflict + destination day. Mark real vs imagined and known-to-reader vs known-to-character.
STAGE 3 — Part division: split by real day shift, POV switch, encounter boundary, location transition, or meaningful cliffhanger. Assign provisional Part 1/2/3... titles; part numbers are editorial until Director locks them. No automatic time jump or multiple unrelated households in one part without authorization.
STAGE 4 — Scene division: scene IDs E04-Pxx-Syy; one scene = one place, one practical objective, a defined start state, one sequence of actions/conversation, a meaningful outcome and an exit into the next. Split long source windows into A/B subbeats if required, but do not lose any named beat or its outcome.
STAGE 5 — Ask DIRECTOR for only the immediately blocking choice, one at a time. First produce source map and a recommended scope, then stop. Do not immediately dump full episode prose or 25 Grok prompts.
STAGE 6 — Handover: after Director explicitly approves an individual scene brief, generate ONE GROK HANDOVER ONLY for that scene; no handover for unapproved scenes.
STAGE 7 — Review: after Grok draft is pasted, compare it to RAW DIRECTOR INPUT and approved map line by line. Flag omitted events, unwanted inventions, chronology, wrong characters, accidental vs intentional actions, changed knowledge, missing transition, duplicate incident, confused consent/intent, and drift. Give exact correction tickets with draft line IDs. Don't secretly rewrite the scene or overrule Director.
STAGE 8 — Archive/publish: after my approval, preserve canonical text, store version and status, update timeline/UI and links. Never mark a draft published just because it exists; likewise if I explicitly direct AS-IS publication, upload it without imposing optional continuity corrections.
