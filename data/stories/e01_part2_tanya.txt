S|Saturday 4:32 PM - The Courtyard clubhouse
N|By late afternoon, the wedding entrance was finished and the music rehearsal was nearly done. At the clubhouse, Tanya Arora found coloured game cards spread across a table. Four familiar nineteen-year-olds were arguing over tomorrow's wedding games for Anika and Sahil.
D|Tanya|Wonderful. Main games review karne aayi thi aur yahan college canteen ka extension mil gaya.
D|Laksh|Bhabhi, college canteen mein itne achche markers nahi milte.
D|Vihaan|Thirty-six question cards banaye hain. Categories approve kar do.
D|Tanya|Thirty-six? Wedding game hai, UPSC exam nahi.
N|Vihaan Arora was Dev's younger cousin, and Tanya's cousin-devar. Arjun Mehta was Samar's cousin and Shreya's devar. Dhruv Sinha was Aditya's cousin and Shivani's devar. Laksh Bansal was Ritvik's cousin and Ankita's devar. The four were close friends, studying BBA with marketing projects at the same college. They knew all four married bhabhis through extended-family gatherings, but Tanya had become their favourite.
N|Vihaan liked taking charge; Arjun had a joke and a style opinion for every occasion; Dhruv was quiet and observant; Laksh drew cartoons even during serious work. Tanya had been guiding their college marketing presentations for six months, so the boys did not need formal introductions or a reason to feel comfortable with her.
D|Arjun|Bhabhi, aaj aapka look toh hamare campaign posters se bhi better hai.
D|Tanya|Toh poster improve karo. Compliment se poor product nahi bachega.
D|Laksh|One compliment, one performance review. Classic Tanya bhabhi.
N|Tanya laughed and examined a card. In her work as a marketing consultant, she helped businesses understand their audience, choose messages and plan campaigns. She had taught the boys that a successful campaign did not treat people as numbers.
D|Tanya|Ek baat pehle clear karo. Guests ko hasana hai ya unki personal life ko stage par expose karna hai?
D|Dhruv|Humne private questions remove kiye. Har card pe pass option bhi rakhenge.
D|Tanya|Good. Jo comfortable nahi, unko reason dene ki bhi zaroorat nahi.
N|Ira Arora entered with lemonade. She was twenty-nine, Dev's younger sister and Tanya's nanad. Ira was quieter than Tanya in public, but she knew exactly how to tease her bhabhi.
D|Ira|Marketing class chal rahi hai ya favourite-bhabhi appreciation meeting?
D|Tanya|Tu meri side par hai ya inki?
D|Ira|Sach ki side par. Arjun ne tareef ki aur tumne uska campaign reject kar diya.
N|Everyone laughed. Tanya looked stern for another second and then took a glass. These were familiar younger family members; their easy humour had grown over years of gatherings and the recent college project.
H|Tanya|Tanya liked their energy because she remembered how uncertain they had been when she began mentoring them. The boys felt like family, friends and eager students of her advice. Their compliments amused her, but she did not know the more personal emotions some of them were keeping private.
S|Saturday 4:48 PM - Rewriting the wedding games
N|The first card asked couples to reveal an embarrassing habit of their partners. Tanya crossed out the question and wrote a new one: Tell us one small kindness your partner does that others might not know. Vihaan nodded and rewrote the category.
D|Vihaan|Same fun, but more positive. Yeh better hai.
D|Tanya|Exactly. Wedding guests audience hain, marketing leads nahi.
D|Arjun|Most stylish guest ka award bhi rakhein? Mere paas perfect nominee hai.
D|Ira|Apna naam likh diya hoga.
D|Arjun|Nahi. Tanya bhabhi.
D|Tanya|Rejected. Family loyalty is not audience research.
N|Six months earlier, at their college marketing festival, Vihaan had rushed through the slides, Arjun had overfocused on taglines, Dhruv's research had been buried and Laksh had filled awkward gaps with jokes. Their mock pitch went badly.
N|Rather than fixing it for them, Tanya invited all four to her dining table over two weekends. She made them explain the customer problem, rehearse objections and give each other feedback. They earned an improved final presentation and, more importantly, enough confidence to challenge each other constructively.
D|Vihaan|Bhabhi, aapne humara first pitch bachaya tha. Kal ke games bhi aap hi rescue karogi.
D|Tanya|Rescue unko milti hai jo pehle khud kaam karte hain. Ab questions khatam karo.
H|Vihaan|Vihaan had admired Tanya since she helped him recover from that poor presentation without humiliating him. Her confident way of speaking and her attention to quieter teammates had become personally attractive to him. He sometimes found a genuine extra question just to remain near her a little longer. Tanya saw an improving young leader, not his concealed crush.
N|Dhruv checked another prompt about first impressions and suggested the option to skip if guests considered it too personal.
D|Dhruv|Host ko pass bolne wale se reason bhi nahi poochhna chahiye.
D|Tanya|Perfect. Is rule ko bold mein sabse upar rakho.
D|Laksh|Dhruv ka quote college noticeboard par jayega.
D|Dhruv|Spelling check kar lena.
N|Tanya smiled at Dhruv. He rarely demanded attention, but his quiet observations often prevented the loudest members from making bad decisions.
H|Dhruv|Dhruv noticed the considerate side of Tanya that sat beneath her quick jokes. During their old college rehearsals she had always checked whether the shyest student was comfortable. He liked her for that thoughtfulness and wanted his work to earn her respect. He had never admitted how personal his admiration felt; she knew him as a quiet, dependable younger cousin.
S|Saturday 5:08 PM - Trial round
N|Ira appointed herself judge, Vihaan took the timer, Arjun became mock host and Laksh played a pretend guest. Before Tanya could escape, they seated her in the middle as a test participant.
D|Tanya|Main host hoon. Tum mujhe contestant kyun bana rahe ho?
D|Laksh|User testing, bhabhi. Apna product khud test karna hota hai.
D|Ira|I hate to admit it, but this is sound marketing logic.
D|Arjun|First prompt. Ek person ki quality jo genuinely impress karti hai. Main bolun? Tanya bhabhi kabhi kisi ko stupid feel nahi hone deti.
N|This time Arjun sounded completely sincere. Tanya, usually ready with a comeback, paused for a second.
D|Tanya|Unexpectedly sensible. Tumhe points milenge.
D|Arjun|Kabhi-kabhi real compliment bhi de sakta hoon, bhabhi.
D|Tanya|Bas roz ka quota mat banana.
H|Arjun|Arjun often praised Tanya's style with teasing confidence, but his fondness was not only about her appearance. She let him be silly without dismissing his serious effort, and he treasured that attention. Today he wanted to thank her genuinely. Tanya appreciated his words but did not recognise the private attraction behind them.
N|Laksh turned around a hand-drawn card. It showed a stern Tanya in the middle of four helpless students carrying colourful posters.
D|Ira|Yeh Tanya bhabhi hai ya school principal?
D|Laksh|Favourite bhabhi, marketing mentor and unpaid creative director.
D|Tanya|Unpaid? Tumhari rehearsal ki chai mein main fully compensated thi.
D|Laksh|Next project mein bhi chai chalegi?
D|Tanya|Next project mein completed assignment chahiye. Chai bonus.
N|Laksh grinned and drew an even funnier cartoon, just to hear Tanya laugh again.
H|Laksh|Laksh enjoyed her witty replies more than winning an argument. His jokes had become a way to seek a few seconds of her attention, a private attraction he barely understood himself. For Tanya he remained the mischievous young cousin who needed deadlines as much as encouragement.
N|They shortened thirty-six cards to twelve. Dhruv finalised the rules, Vihaan checked timing, Arjun sorted colour labels and Laksh tested the timer. The wedding game now had a workable plan rather than a list of embarrassing dares.
S|Saturday 5:25 PM - Balcony and final checklist
N|While the boys scanned the final cards, Ira took Tanya onto the clubhouse balcony. Society lights were coming on below them. Tanya leaned against the railing, grateful for two quiet minutes.
D|Ira|Tum un chaaron ke saath itni patience kaise rakhti ho?
D|Tanya|Mera first marketing pitch yaad hai? Team ne bina sune reject kiya tha. Main ghar aakar royi thi. Agle din main phir prepare karke gayi.
D|Ira|Isliye tum inko seriously leti ho, even jab woh silly behave karte hain.
D|Tanya|Haan. Lekin homework maaf nahi.
N|Ira laughed. She was shy in larger company, yet with Tanya she knew when a thoughtful comment could reach further than a joke.
D|Ira|Waise Arjun ki sincere tareef pe tum ek second chup ho gayi thi.
D|Tanya|Main shock mein thi ki usne joke ke bina poora sentence bola.
D|Ira|Of course, bhabhi. Of course.
N|Tanya bumped Ira gently with her shoulder. Their quiet family closeness was different from the loud energy inside the clubhouse.
H|Tanya|Tanya enjoyed being called their favourite bhabhi, but she saw four family-connected young adults whose confidence she had helped build. Their compliments sometimes made her laugh and sometimes moved her. She had not guessed the different concealed interests behind them, and she had not made any romantic choice.
N|They returned to the room. Tanya approved the twelve-card set and marked PASS IS ALWAYS OKAY at the top of the instructions. The four boys confirmed their wedding-day responsibilities. Ira gathered the lemonade glasses.
D|Tanya|Kal guests ko fun feel hona chahiye, awkward nahi. Favourite bhabhi ka title free mein nahi milta. Time par aana.
D|Vihaan|Yes, coach.
D|Arjun|Yes, bhabhi.
D|Dhruv|Rules final.
D|Laksh|Jokes ka guarantee nahi.
N|Their laughter filled the clubhouse. The family bond remained unchanged to everyone present. Only the reader knew how different the four boys' inner responses were. Episode 01, Part 2 - Tanya ended here.
