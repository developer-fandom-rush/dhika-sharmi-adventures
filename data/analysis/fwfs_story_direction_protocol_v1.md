# FOUR WALLS, FOUR SECRETS — Story Direction & Writing Handoff Protocol v1
**Status:** E01-derived working guide, NOT final canon, and NOT a claim that the user's writing instructions or scene decisions are approved beyond what is separately recorded.

## Governing roles — explicitly agreed
- **Reader / Creative authority (user):** Can accept, reject or rewrite any candidate scene beat. A GPT proposal is NEVER automatically canon. Neither GPT nor Grok may silently change an approved scene.
- **GPT — Director and source analyst, not the fiction writer:** Read the original DHIKA source, divide it into meaningful source-backed sections, compare mechanisms, track continuity, brainstorm **short non-graphic labels** and options, inspect constraints, and prepare step-by-step Grok briefs ONLY after reader selects a path.
- **Grok — prose writer:** Given an approved, versioned brief, creates full original FWFS narrative in its own output. It must not add unapproved relationship milestones. Return prose for independent review; never call it approved merely because generated.
- **Independent ChatGPT review session/model — quality auditor:** Re-fetch E01 artefacts, distinguish draft versions, report concrete issues with citations, propose fixes, DO NOT directly rewrite or overwrite canon.
- **Consent/agency/observer knowledge:** Track separately for every scene. Embarrassment, body language, private attraction, dialogue or accidental contact must not be written as proof of consent or mutual intention. Hidden violations are not validated by a character's rationalisation.
- **No explicit graphic adult content in GPT plans.** The director may use concise action labels, doers, observers, reactions, consequences and highest boundary attained. Detailed sexualised scene choreography is not part of the planning deliverable.

## Observed E01 FWFS prose DNA (for Grok, not to write directly now)
1. **Roman Hindi/Hinglish** with simple English where natural, concrete task-led settings and modestly paced dialogue. Avoid long generic emotional summaries substituting for depicted actions.
2. **Four distinct voices:** Ankita = analytical, witty and professional; Shivani = quiet, observant, careful and financial/educational context; Shreya = creative, musical, lightly playful; Tanya = outspoken, organised and bantering with four devars; Chirag/Chotu (22) = earnest delivery worker, thoughtful and sometimes hesitant.
3. **Scene engine:** ordinary reason for encounter → named initiator → dialogue and clear action label → a surprising shift/reaction → separate inner interpretations → meaningful practical outcome/exit → unanswered question. Repetition must develop the relationship rather than give four clones.
4. **Specificity:** Track time, location, speaker, task, physical presence (non-graphic labels at GPT planning stage), actual observed behaviour and who heard it. A character never knows another's private thought without credible evidence.
5. **Continuity:** each woman's family, spouse, existing relationship, attire, professional obligations, previous story encounters and original cast names are stable. Source's characters are not interchangeable with FWFS's characters.
6. **Tension without explicitness at planning stage:** ambiguous gaze, compliment, silence, conflicting inner feeling, an intentional choice, a real accident and private aftermath can be labelled. Never mistake a planned outfit for permission to touch or make all hidden motives identical.
7. **Reader-only private layer:** Mark deliberate character actions, concealment, wrong assumptions and independent knowledge explicitly; keep hidden elder conduct from Part 3 *earlier than* Chirag's Part 5 entry. Chirag is NOT the person who took or displaced those garments.
8. **Ending:** track the real result of the ordinary task AND emotional change; never end by claiming unresolved mysteries are solved.
9. **Originality:** Refer to DHIKA as a **writing mechanism** (e.g. everyday errand -> interruption -> asymmetrical knowledge), not as text to paraphrase sentence by sentence. Distinct original scenes and idiom.

## Required Episode 02 director action sheet — one row per candidate
`source_line_range | source_fact_label | FWFS_candidate_ID | ordinary_setting | doer | affected_character | observer | action_label | immediate_reaction | reader_only_knowledge | highest_boundary_attained | aftermath | practical_exit | timeline_dependency | continuity_risk | approved_status`
- **Highest boundary attained**: descriptive NON-GRAPHIC milestone such as *first noticed*, *direct compliment*, *asked for help*, *momentary proximity*, *disclosed secret*, *accepted/refused invitation*, *trust repaired*, or *genuine boundary crossed*. Never a measurement or choreography.
- Each action must be grounded in FWFS reality; DHIKA source range only supports the storytelling-mechanism reference, not that the FWFS event already happened.
- For each part, propose 2-3 **genuinely different** scene alternatives with small consequence chains; recommend one with reasons, and let the reader decide.
- Summarise what cannot happen yet because of timestamps, unresolved relationships, missing prose, or lack of consent.

## Approval / handoff state machine
`SOURCE VERIFIED → SOURCE BLOCKS REVIEWED → ACTION OPTIONS PROPOSED → READER SELECTED → DIRECTOR BRIEF LOCKED → GROK DRAFT RECEIVED → INDEPENDENT QA → READER APPROVED → CANON`.
Never promote a status by inference, and never change a story manuscript or repository file without the user's request or explicit approval.

## Grok handover format — generated only for selected beats
1. Exact FWFS episode/part, desired length and preceding canon references.
2. Complete cast list and non-participant exclusions.
3. Current timestamps and locations; current attire (only what reader has selected).
4. Scene objective and everyday practical reason.
5. Numbered **approved** beats, each with action label, doer, affected party, reaction, observed consequence, hidden knowledge, physical/consent boundaries if relevant.
6. Character dialogue signatures and sample **original** non-graphic one-liners if approved; do not import original DHIKA prose verbatim.
7. Expected exit conditions, unresolved threads, next-scene dependency and prohibitions (no new cast, no extra physical milestone, no skipped consequence, no past-event retcon).
8. Required output: Grok's full original fictional prose, self-QA checklist, and a delta report identifying anything it could not implement.
9. Never mark a generated draft as canon; return it to GPT auditor/user for review.
